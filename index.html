01

Understanding ProRes vs Apple Log

These are two separate things that get confused often. They do different jobs.

Codec

ProRes

The Quality of the Container

Normal Recording (HEVC)

Compresses aggressively. ~350 MB/min at 4K. Push colors in editing and it falls apart — banding, artifacts, mushy detail.

Think JPEG

ProRes

Barely compresses. ~6 GB/min at 4K. Color grade holds up beautifully — smooth gradients, clean detail.

Think RAW

Tradeoff: Massive files. Requires external USB-C SSD.

Color Profile

Apple Log

How Light and Color Are Recorded

Standard Mode

iPhone processes in real time — boosting contrast, saturating, sharpening. Looks great but baked in. Blown sky? Gone forever.

Apple Log

Records flat, grey image on purpose. Preserves all detail in highlights and shadows. Magic happens in post-production.

Analogy

Standard = Fully cooked meal at your table.

Apple Log = Raw ingredients — you control every flavor.

How They Combine

HEVCStandard~350 MB/minLowQuick casual
HEVCApple LogSmall–medHighSocial & web (recommended)

ProResHDRHugeMediumHQ without grading
02

Recommended Recording Format

The Sweet Spot

HEVC + Apple Log

For social media and website videos

01

Platforms recompress on upload. HEVC Log vs ProRes Log difference is zero after compression.

02

10–15x smaller files than ProRes. Storage lasts much longer.

03

Editing in VN/CapCut stays fast — files aren't massive.

04

Full flat Log image with complete grading flexibility.

05

No external SSD required. Blackmagic Camera's biggest advantage over native app.

When to consider ProRes

  • Film festival or broadcast delivery
  • Desktop editing (DaVinci Resolve)
  • Heavy compositing or VFX
  • Large displays where every pixel matters

Blackmagic Camera Settings Explained

Frame Rate (FPS)

How many images the camera captures per second.

24fps

Cinema standard. Motion blur. "Movie" feel.

Your Default
25fps

European broadcast (PAL).

30fps

Smoother. News, vlogs, talking-head.

60fps

Slow motion at 24 fps playback.

Slow-Mo

Shutter Speed

180° Shutter Rule

Double your frame rate: 24fps → 1/50  |  30fps → 1/60  |  60fps → 1/120

Too fast (1/500 at 24fps) = Jittery, staccato. A creative choice, not default.

Too slow = Excessive blur and smearing.

On tripod, shutter still controls exposure (brightness).

ISO

Sensor sensitivity. Volume knob for brightness.

50–100
Cleanest — bright daylight
200–400
Very clean — overcast, shade
400–800
Slight grain — dim interiors
1600+
Noisy — avoid, add light
Lowest ISO possible. Use lighting and shutter speed before raising ISO.

White Balance (Kelvin)

2700KCandle
3200KTungsten
4000KFluoro
5000KMidday
5600KDaylight
6500KOvercast
7500K+Shade
Critical

Always lock white balance manually. Auto WB shifts mid-shot — colors drift between clips. Set once per scene.

Focus & Exposure

Manual Focus

Lock focus on subject. Turn on Focus Peaking — highlights in-focus edges.

Zebras

Stripes on overexposed areas. If sky shows zebras, darken exposure.

LUT Preview

Live preview overlay. Recording stays flat — LUT is display-only. Essential for judging exposure.

Expose for highlights. Easier to brighten shadows than recover blown highlights.

Composition & Framing

Rule of Thirds

Grid overlay on. Place subject on intersection points — not dead center.

Leading Lines

Roads, fences, rivers — guide the viewer's eye toward your subject.

Foreground Depth

Shoot through doorways, branches, arches. Layers create 3D feel.

Symmetry

Center framing creates tension. Great for architecture, tunnels.

Negative Space

Empty space around subject = scale, isolation, calm.

Vary Your Shots

Wide, medium, close-up, detail, high angle, low angle. Edit needs options.

06

Lighting Tips

Golden Hour

#1 factor in cinematic footage. First hour after sunrise, last before sunset. Warm, soft, directional.

Blue Hour

20–30 min before sunrise / after sunset. Cool, moody, even. Beautiful for cityscapes.

Backlighting

Light behind subject = rim light + separation from background.

Overcast = Soft Light

Natural softbox. Smooth skin, gentle shadows, even colors.

Avoid Harsh Midday Sun

Hard shadows, squinting, blown highlights. Find shade or wait for clouds.

Expose for highlights. Shadows recoverable. Blown highlights are not.

08

LUTs What & How

A LUT (.cube file) remaps every color in your footage to a new value.

Technical

Conversion LUT

Log → Rec.709. Normalizes flat footage. Like developing a film negative.

Creative

Creative LUT

Applied after conversion. Gives mood — warm cinematic, teal & orange, vintage.

Where to Get LUTs

Phantom LUTs

~$30–50

Calibrated against ARRI Alexa. DJI D-Log matching.

FilterGrade

Free

8 free .cube files, works in VN + LumaFusion.

IWLTBAP

~$40

325+ cinematic LUTs, all platforms.

LUT Studio

Free/Sub

90+ built-in, Apple Log support.

Best Practices

Color correct FIRST (white balance, exposure) before creative LUT
Conversion LUT first, creative LUT on top
Never 100% intensity — dial back for natural look
Same LUT across all clips for consistency

14

Quick Reminders

01

Apple Log looks ugly on purpose. That flat grey image holds all the detail. Trust the process.

02

Lock white balance and focus. Every time. No exceptions.

03

Expose for highlights. Shadows recoverable. Blown highlights are not.

04

ISO as low as possible. Add light before adding ISO.

05

Hold shots 5+ extra seconds. You'll thank yourself in the edit.

06

Shoot variety. Wide, medium, close-up, detail. The edit needs options.

07

Grade before LUTs. Fix basics first.

08

LUTs at 60–80%. Full strength looks overdone.

09

Music first, edit second. Let the track guide pacing.

10

HEVC + Apple Log. ProRes is overkill for social and web.
